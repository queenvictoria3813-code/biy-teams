Invention Universe
 Project Management Site

Explore Invention Universe.  You collect gold coins for 
1) creating your planet, 
2) adding projects,
3) solving challenges, and
4) mentoring others.

 

With your gold coins, you can design or 'buy' a rocket to go faster and further.

You will be able to order parts, tools and supplies from a fulfillment partner.

 

Each Build-It-Yourself member can create his or her own planet.

 

A member's planet may have:
1. alien avatars, 
2. creator's profile or spec's and 
3. museum of projects or inventions.

 

When you land on a planet, you are greeted by the member's avatar alien.

 

Sign up for live Webcast workshops that inspire and guide you to create the project of the month.

Earn gold coins that can be cashed in for parts, tools or other prizes!

Invention Universe is a building community for playful inventors that is inspired by YouTube, MySpace and the computer game Spore.

Build-It-Yourself Business Goal
Create a community of 150,000 members who pay $30/month to:

  1. learn new tricks from art and engineering students (live webcasts),
  2. access content, (Build-It-Blocks) and
  3. show off their inventions. (Invention Universe)

Problem Statement

  1. The next generation of leaders and builders must learn how to use technology to solve complex problems.
  2. It is difficult for schools to teach technology due to low budgets, test standards, and teacher training.
  3. Parents and teachers are often not comfortable with some computer games and social web sites that are the main means for kids to be fluent in technology.
  4. In the old days, kids exercised entrepreneurial skills running lemonade stands, delivering newspapers or baby sitting. There is a need to offer the next generation constructive ways of earning money that is relevant to their world.

Proposed Solution

We are developing an online environment that will inspire and guide kids to build and be fluent in technology.  The Build-It-Yourself platform will enable kids to:

  1. Build a portfolio of projects that can be used in school applications.
  2. Form friendships and share ideas with builders from around the world;
  3. Collaborate with specialists many of whom are model college students who are on promising tracks.
  4. Win awards and earn money. (earn points as in Karate belts or Boy Scout badges, become a Build-It-Yourself instructor, recruit members)
  5. Exercise graphic design, computer, problem solving and presentation skills.
  6. Build playful, engaging projects in less than 12 hours.

Target Audience

  1. Kids 8-13 who like to build and spend time on computers.
  2. Parents who are eager for their kids to learn skills that will help them be successful.
  3. After-school program directors
  4. BIY administration

Kids 8-13

  1. Upload projects(physical and virtual)
  2. Play virtual projects
  3. Manage her own projects
  4. Browse/search her own and other kids' projects
  5. Create/manage her own profile page (portfolios in a form presentable to schools admission officers)
  6. Communicate/collaborate with other kids and BIY staff
  7. Participate in online classes taught by BIY staff
  8. Access project instructions, self-help tutorials and materials
  9. Comment and rate other kids' projects

Parents

  1. Access their children's profile pages (and have permission to edit)
  2. Schedule workshops
  3. Communicate with BIY staff

After-school Program Directors

  1. Access their students' profiles (and have permission to edit)
  2. Schedule workshops
  3. Communicate with BIY staff

BIY Administration

  1. Manage content
  2. Grade/evaluate class projects and student performance
  3. Manage students' projects
  4. Communicate with students, parents and school administrators
  5. Give awards
  6. Manage comments

Short Term Development Goals: 

  1. When demonstrated to 10 kids in the target age group, 3 will explore the Invention Universe for 10 minutes.
  2. 30% of parents polled in our survey say that the game would be a somewhat important or very important part of their decision to pay for a BIY Membership.
  3. Set up and document the infrastructure, procedures, and code so the team can work efficiently from remote locations.
  4. Document the code so an MIT computer science major can  make edits to the project after only 1 day of studying the code.
